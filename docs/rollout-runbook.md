# Real-time adjudication rollout runbook

## Guardrails and flag contract

Real-time adjudication is introduced through one mutually exclusive `ADJUDICATION_STAGE` flag. Allowed values, in promotion order, are:

`LOCAL_VALIDATION`, `TEST_PARITY`, `PRODUCTION_SHADOW`, `INTERNAL_PILOT`, `LIMITED_DECISIONS`, and `FULL_ADOPTION`.

The deployment platform must reject an unknown value. Changing the flag requires a change record, the approval of the accountable owner named below, a dashboard link proving the entry criteria, and a recorded rollback decision-maker. Flag changes must be independently configurable by environment and must not require a new application build.

Two supporting controls constrain production exposure:

- `REALTIME_DECISION_PERCENT` is `0` through shadow mode, is restricted to an allowlist in the internal pilot, is at most `10` in limited decisions, and may reach `100` only at full adoption. Assignment is deterministic by claim ID so retries cannot change paths.
- `REALTIME_PILOT_ALLOWLIST` contains internal test submitters or explicitly enrolled providers. Empty means no allowlisted traffic, never all traffic.

An **eligible comparison** is a claim for which both systems received the same normalized input and all required database, eligibility, and AS400 evidence was available. Decision parity is the percentage of eligible comparisons with identical final status and reason codes. Monetary parity is the percentage with identical payable amount. Availability excludes planned maintenance; latency is measured end to end at the API boundary. Targets are evaluated over the stated observation window with at least 1,000 eligible comparisons unless a stage explicitly uses a smaller controlled set.

## Stages and promotion gates

| Stage and behavior | Entry criteria | Measurable parity and reliability target | Rollback / stop condition | Accountable owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Local validation** (`LOCAL_VALIDATION`). Developer workstations and CI use fixtures only; decisions cannot be transmitted to a claim system. | Unit, contract, and failure-injection suites exist; fixture provenance is documented; logs include claim ID, trace ID, stage, evidence timestamps, and decision reasons with protected data redacted. | 100% expected status/reason parity on the curated golden set; 100% of dependency-failure cases produce `PENDED` or a documented retryable error; all automated tests pass in three consecutive CI runs. | Any silent approval without required evidence, golden-case mismatch, nondeterministic retry result, or protected data in logs blocks promotion. Revert the code/config change and remain local. | **Adjudication engineering lead** |
| **2. Test-environment parity** (`TEST_PARITY`). The service reads production-like test integrations but remains non-authoritative. | Stage 1 signed off; sanitized batch baseline loaded; database, eligibility, and AS400 test contracts verified; dashboards and paging routes exercised. | At least 99.5% decision parity and 99.9% monetary parity over 5 consecutive business days; at least 99.9% availability; p95 latency <= 2 s and p99 <= 5 s; zero unsafe approvals. Every mismatch is classified. | Parity below target for two runs, availability below target in a rolling 24 h window, p99 > 5 s for 30 min, any unsafe approval, or an unclassified mismatch older than 1 business day. Set traffic to the existing batch path and return to Stage 1 diagnostics. | **QA/test lead** |
| **3. Production shadow** (`PRODUCTION_SHADOW`, decision percent `0`). Real-time evaluates a copy of production traffic; only the batch result is authoritative. Nightly reconciliation is mandatory. | Stage 2 signed off; production read-only access and capacity review complete; on-call runbook and rollback exercised; privacy/security approval recorded; comparison events are durable and idempotent. | At least 99.8% decision parity and 99.95% monetary parity for 14 consecutive days; at least 99.95% availability; p95 <= 1.5 s and p99 <= 4 s; reconciliation covers 100% of received claims by 09:00 local operations time; zero unsafe approvals (even though shadow results are not served). | Any unsafe hypothetical approval, missed reconciliation, comparison coverage < 99.9%, parity below target on any two nightly runs, availability below target for 30 min, or critical data-quality incident. Disable shadow evaluation; batch remains authoritative. | **Production operations lead** |
| **4. Internal pilot** (`INTERNAL_PILOT`). Only allowlisted internal/synthetic claims receive real-time decisions; all other claims use batch. Nightly reconciliation remains mandatory and batch can correct pilot outcomes before downstream payment. | Stage 3 signed off; 14-day target met; allowlist reviewed; support and claims operations trained; correction and claimant-notification procedures rehearsed; pilot volume/cost limits configured. | At least 99.9% decision parity and 99.99% monetary parity for 7 consecutive days; at least 99.95% availability; p95 <= 1.5 s and p99 <= 4 s; 100% nightly reconciliation by 09:00; zero unsafe approvals; at least 99% of retryable failures resolve within one nightly cycle. | Any unsafe approval, monetary mismatch, reconciliation miss, Sev-1/Sev-2 incident attributable to real time, parity below target, or retry backlog older than one business day. Immediately clear the allowlist and use batch for every claim. | **Claims operations director** |
| **5. Limited real-time decisions** (`LIMITED_DECISIONS`, deterministic exposure <= 10%). Selected external claims receive authoritative real-time decisions; exclusions and remaining traffic use batch. Nightly reconciliation remains mandatory. | Stage 4 signed off; 7-day target met; compliance and business approvals recorded; provider cohort and maximum exposure documented; 24x7 on-call and automated kill switch verified; no open high-severity mismatches. | At least 99.95% decision parity and 99.995% monetary parity for 14 consecutive days; at least 99.97% availability; p95 <= 1 s and p99 <= 3 s; 100% nightly reconciliation by 09:00; zero unsafe approvals; reconciliation alert acknowledged within 15 min and resolved/classified within 4 h. | Any unsafe approval or confirmed overpayment, monetary parity below target, decision parity below target on two consecutive windows, reconciliation incomplete at deadline, availability below 99.9% over 1 h, p99 > 3 s for 30 min, or retry queue beyond capacity. Set percent to `0`; batch becomes authoritative, and reconcile all exposed claims before re-entry. | **VP of claims (business service owner)** |
| **6. Full adoption** (`FULL_ADOPTION`, exposure up to 100%). Real time is authoritative; batch is retained as a recovery path until formally retired. | Stage 5 signed off after at least 30 days at the maximum limited cohort; capacity/load and disaster-recovery tests pass at 2x forecast peak; audit/compliance approve; no unresolved Sev-1/Sev-2 issue; executive service owner accepts residual risk. | At least 99.95% decision parity and 99.995% monetary parity during a 30-day ramp; at least 99.99% availability; p95 <= 1 s and p99 <= 3 s; zero unsafe approvals. Reconciliation remains daily for the first 30 days, then may be reduced only through a separate approved control change. | Any unsafe approval/overpayment, availability below 99.9% over 1 h, sustained latency breach for 30 min, material parity regression, evidence-integrity incident, or disaster-recovery failure. Reduce exposure to `0` and restore batch authority; incident commander decides any gradual re-entry. | **VP of claims (business service owner)** |

Targets are gates, not error budgets that permit unsafe approvals. Security, compliance, or incident command may halt a stage regardless of the numeric targets.

## Reconciliation and difference alerts

For `PRODUCTION_SHADOW`, `INTERNAL_PILOT`, and `LIMITED_DECISIONS`, retain the existing nightly batch adjudication and compare it with every real-time outcome. During the first 30 days of `FULL_ADOPTION`, run the same reconciliation daily. Keep immutable inputs, evidence versions/timestamps, rule-set versions, statuses, reason codes, payable amounts, stage, and trace IDs so a difference can be reproduced without relying on mutable source records.

The reconciliation job must:

1. prove completeness by comparing source intake, real-time decision, and batch control totals;
2. classify differences as input/evidence timing, rule/version, integration failure, or true logic mismatch;
3. emit a high-severity alert immediately for an `APPROVED` real-time result when batch returns `DENIED`, `REVIEW`, or `PENDED`, or for any payable-amount difference;
4. emit a warning for all other status or reason-code differences and for missing comparison records;
5. page the stage owner and production on-call for high-severity alerts, create an incident linked by trace ID, and acknowledge within 15 minutes; and
6. preserve results for the audit retention period and track correction through closure.

No discrepancy is resolved merely by rerunning or overwriting it. A reviewer must record the authoritative result, root-cause category, claimant/payment impact, remediation, and whether rollback criteria were met.

## Dependency failure behavior

An approval requires current, affirmative evidence from every mandatory dependency. Cached, partial, default, or missing evidence must never be interpreted as eligible or as “no duplicate found.” The API either returns a decision with status `PENDED` and a stable reason code, or a clearly retryable error when no durable claim/decision record can be created. Callers must receive the same result for retries with the same idempotency key.

| Failure | Required behavior | Recovery and observability |
|---|---|---|
| **Claims database outage or failed write** | If the request and a `PENDED` decision can be durably recorded in an independent queue/store, return `PENDED` with `DB-RETRY`. If durability or idempotency cannot be guaranteed, return HTTP `503 Service Unavailable` with error code `DB_UNAVAILABLE`, `Retry-After`, and the trace ID. Never return `APPROVED`. | Use bounded retries with jitter and a circuit breaker; do not retry past the request deadline. Page database on-call, expose queue age/depth, and replay idempotently after recovery. Reconcile replayed decisions that reached a production stage. |
| **AS400 unavailable, malformed response, or integration failure** | Return `PENDED` with `AS400-RETRY` after a durable pend is recorded; otherwise return retryable HTTP `503` with `AS400_UNAVAILABLE`. A failed/no-result lookup is not evidence that no duplicate exists. | Open the circuit after the configured threshold, alert integration on-call, record response/version metadata without sensitive payloads, retry asynchronously, and route aged pends to operations. Batch remains the recovery authority while retained. |
| **Timeout (dependency or total request deadline)** | Cancel remaining work. Return durably recorded `PENDED` with `TIMEOUT-RETRY`, or HTTP `504 Gateway Timeout` with `TIMEOUT`, `Retry-After`, and trace ID if it could not be recorded. Do not continue and later publish a contradictory result without reconciliation. | Apply dependency-specific deadlines shorter than the API deadline, bounded retries only when time remains, latency/error-rate alerts, and idempotent completion from the pending queue. |
| **Stale or missing eligibility data** | Evidence older than the configured business-approved maximum age, lacking an as-of timestamp, or predating a relevant coverage change is unusable. Return `PENDED` with `ELIGIBILITY-STALE`; if the eligibility service cannot refresh it and no durable pend is possible, return retryable HTTP `503` with `ELIGIBILITY_UNAVAILABLE`. Never infer eligibility from a stale cached value. | Include evidence age and source in metrics, not protected values. Request a refresh, alert when stale rate or oldest pend exceeds threshold, and require fresh evidence before resuming adjudication. |

`PENDED` is non-payable and non-denial: downstream systems must not release payment or tell a claimant that the claim was denied. Pending work has an explicit owner, next-attempt time, retry count, and maximum age. Exhausting automated retries routes the claim to manual operations review; it does not convert the outcome to `APPROVED`. Validation/authentication errors remain non-retryable `4xx` responses and are not converted to `PENDED`.
